# Editorial Introduction: Song of Enlightenment

The present edition publishes a poem-first critical text of the `永嘉證道歌`, traditionally attributed to Yongjia Xuanjue. The package was opened as a bounded follow-up to the `Faith in Mind` critical edition, but from the start its editorial posture was stricter: exact poem witnesses first, secondary controls second, commentary never allowed to sprawl into a parallel main workstream.

That decision proved correct. The edition now rests on a substantial exact witness field rather than on a single anthology or a single clean modern surrogate. `YJG-W22` was selected as the copy-text because it is the earliest complete independent exact witness already held as a full local image tranche and directly usable inside the active comparison workflow. At the same time, the edition does not pretend that copy-text selection solves every local page problem. The accepted poem body depends heavily on cleaner shared-exact interiors in `YJG-W16` and `YJG-W17`, and the later OCR-complete first-tier controls serve mainly to reinforce opening, closing, and witness-class judgments rather than to drive another hidden round of text change.

The result is intentionally restrained. This is not a full witness-by-witness collation of every held manifestation, nor a commentary edition. It is a poem-first critical edition with a selective apparatus and a fully logged process. The package preserves a large amount of supporting evidence: witness hunting, OCR across multiple engines, anchor planning, boundary review, rejected readings, translation reasoning, and blocked-source honesty. But the main edition surfaces keep the focus where it belongs, on the poem itself and on the material editorial judgments needed to establish it.

The package also closes with a required philological comparison against prior completed critical editions in the repo. At present that means `Faith in Mind`. The comparison does not show poem-body dependence between the two edited texts, but it does confirm that the broader `Faith in Mind` package repeatedly cites and mines `證道歌` quotation families in its secondary and commentary-facing layers. That result belongs in the record because Zen textuality is strongly intertextual even when two main reading texts do not directly govern one another.

This edition is therefore offered as a publication-form research release candidate: a stable poem text, a selective apparatus, synchronized translation through the current frontier, and a process layer honest enough to show both what was established and what was intentionally not overclaimed.
